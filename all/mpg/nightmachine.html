Overnight Painting Machine

The Overnight Painting Machine was fairly easy to design and build once I figured out what I wanted to do. I had been experimenting with watercolors to see how different colors ran, dripped, and bled on different kinds of papers. I decided to build a machine that had two qualities: 1) the ability to make paintings without me, at a time when I can't be making art; and 2) that used the natural properties of the watercolors and the paper to make images.

To build the machine I used one cricket, a motor, and a light sensor. When the light falls below a certain level, the motor turns on, turns a gear, and lowers the paper (suspended with clips on strings) into the pool of pigmented water. In the morning when the sun comes up, the motor reverses direction and pulls the paper up out of the water.
