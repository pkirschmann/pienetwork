Here's the description of the workshop:

Join an eclectic group of local artists at an informal workshop to experiment with Crickets, a new programmable technology developed by the MIT Media Lab. In this workshop you'll program a Cricket to control motors, lights, or sound and integrate them with traditional art and building materials to create an interactive object. You'll also meet other artists, musicians, and interesting people, and help us think about how we can work with artists in the science museum.

This workshop is hosted by the Learning Technologies Center (LT Center) at the Science Museum of Minnesota. The LT Center is a place, with computer classrooms, offices, and a creative, lively development lab. It’s a team of developers and researchers who explore, discuss, tinker, and analyze their way to some of the most innovative technology applications in informal science education. It’s an approach to applied technology R&D that puts direct experience with natural phenomena first, and uses relationships between people to ignite and sustain excitement for scientific exploration. Finally, Learning Technologies is a department within Science Museum of Minnesota, working with other departments, and other institutions, to fulfill the promise of digital technologies as informal science learning tools.
